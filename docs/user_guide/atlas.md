## What the user prepares

1. **Prepare the geometry request.** Choose the physical page, margins (including
   binding), projection and approximate angular field. The starting edition uses
   ISO B4 landscape (353 × 250 mm), with a chart on each right-hand page.
   Choose overlap between neighbouring sheets, the RA of the common meridian
   for the overview pair, and the total angular width of their shared equatorial
   band. Select an initial region (Orion is the proposed starting region),
   orientation and editorial ordering. Compare candidate field sizes, for example
   30°, 40° and 50°, before adopting one; these are trials, not defaults.
2. **Generate and inspect the design.** The designer resolves centres and coverage
   into JSON. Prepare the separate index presentation input and use the plotter
   to examine coverage, sheet numbers and navigation. Iterate on geometry if
   there are gaps, unsuitable fields or inconvenient page joins. Freeze a design
   revision before substantial curation.
3. **Prepare the index presentation input.** Choose the size of the overview
   figure, margins, labels, colours and layers. Optional layers include stars,
   Milky Way, Magellanic Clouds, constellation lines, boundaries and abbreviations.
   Stars of magnitude 4 or brighter are an initial trial, not an adopted limit.
   These choices do not alter sheet coverage or numbers.
4. **Prepare the publication and curation input.** Choose chart layers, selected
   star names and Bayer labels, magnitude limits, appearance and output formats.
   Supply the object selections and notes for reports, indicating interest for
   naked-eye, binocular and amateur-telescope observation; categories may
   overlap. Unreviewed suitability remains pending, rather than being inferred
   from a magnitude threshold.
5. **Produce and review a small sample first.** Create the first chart/report
   pairs, beginning around Orion and a contrasting region, then refine star
   symbols, labels and report layout. After accepting the sample, generate the
   remaining pairs and curate them chart by chart. Keep unplotted objects indexed
   for future zoom subcharts instead of deleting catalogue entries.

## Coverage and the polar index

Each sheet has a centre on the celestial sphere defining its tangent plane,
an orientation, a projection and a useful rectangular viewport. Its spherical
footprint follows from projecting that rectangle; it is not an arbitrary
rectangle of RA and declination. The approximate field size, projection and
page aspect ratio jointly determine its dimensions. Individual sheets overlap
and cover the entire sphere, including two actual polar atlas sheets.

The two **polar overview maps** are separate from those polar atlas sheets.
Their common meridian runs through the centre of the shared area; the opposite
meridian is 12 hours away. The shared band width is a total angular width measured
along that meridian. With a symmetric example width of 20°, the northern overview
extends to declination −10° and the southern overview to +10°. This is an example,
not a default. The shared band, neighbouring-sheet overlap and physical overlap
of the two overview shapes on paper are three different quantities.

The plotter should show the overview maps side by side with their inner contours
clipped/composed across the common area. The roughly one-third shared appearance
is a visual goal to assess in a prototype, not a fixed conversion from angular
width to paper spacing. Index borders show each sheet's primary area without its
overlap; the actual sheet footprint includes that overlap. A sheet appearing in
both overviews has the same number.

Individual sheet projection remains a decision to compare. A tangent plane
alone does not choose between stereographic and gnomonic projection. A finite
gnomonic polar sheet cannot extend to the equator. Stereographic projection is
the intended starting point for the hemispheric overviews; it does not settle
the projection or extent of the individual polar atlas sheets.

## What the resolved JSON records

The versioned JSON must contain the resolved design, not only the generator
recipe: schema version and design revision; coordinate frame and applicable
equinox conventions; paper and useful rectangle; sheet identifiers and editorial
numbers; centres, orientations, projections and projected extents; spherical
footprints, primary areas and neighbours; overview common meridian and shared
band; and generator/input provenance. Stellar source epochs are distinct from
coordinate equinox conventions.

Internal identifiers and editorial numbers have different purposes. The
reproducible numbering rule and identifier behaviour after a redesign use the implemented band rule below; arbitrary retiling must not silently
reuse identities.
Coverage validation must detect gaps and measure overlap. Report ownership
should use primary areas, with cross-references for overlap; extended objects
need footprint-aware membership, not just a centre test.

## Changing a finished design

| Change | User action |
|---|---|
| Individual page useful rectangle, angular field, sheet projection, centres or overlap | Revise geometry input and regenerate the JSON; review numbering and coverage |
| Index size, layout, typography, optional astronomical layers or export format | Revise index presentation input and rerun the plotter with the same JSON |
| Selected objects, chart appearance, notes or report categories | Revise publication/curation input and rerun the affected chart/report products |

Each right-hand chart page is paired with a left-hand landscape page containing
the legend, magnitude key, object report and navigation. Chart pages prioritise
astronomical content; selected Bayer labels and optional constellation boundaries
are envisaged. Coordinate furniture remains an editorial decision.

The producer should reuse the existing chart composition and export pipeline,
initially through orchestration of canonical chart requests or `wenu_chart`
calls after an interface audit. There is no separate rendering pipeline planned.

Implementation stages and acceptance gates are recorded in the
[active developer roadmap](../developer/post_v0.9_architecture_roadmap.md#atlas-organisation-checkpoint-2026-10-09).


## Geometry specimen API available for review

The original specimen milestone supplies Python records in
`wenu.atlas_design`, independently of the installed designer described below.
Define a right-hand page
with explicit margins, a common-meridian RA and total shared-band width, and
an explicit list of sheet centres and horizontal field widths. Nonpolar sheets
default to north up and increasing RA to the left; polar sheets require a
meridian orientation. Angular height is derived from the useful paper rectangle
through the projection. This lets us test physical scale without a star catalogue.

`AtlasGeometrySpecimen` can write/read a versioned file such as
`atlas_geometry_specimen_v1.json`. Its document kind explicitly says specimen
and its coverage status is `unverified`; it must not be used as a finished
whole-sky design. The API checks duplicate identities, invalid coordinates,
unknown JSON keys and disagreement between stored and recomputed geometry.
Diagnostic edge samples do not prove full-sky coverage. Primary areas,
neighbours and automatic placement come later. See the
[specimen API reference](../developer/implementation_reference.md#atlas-geometry-specimen-api-candidate).


## Band-coverage comparison API available for review

This next Python prototype can resolve and validate a complete conservative
band layout. The installed designer adapts this same Python API; the plotter and producer
commands remain planned. It does not
produce an index picture or report pages.

Prepare the physical page and margins, a horizontal field, a minimum angular
overlap between sheets and a separate placement seed RA. The overview join RA
and shared band remain independent. For example, the following is a Python
API example, not the future CLI or TOML format:

```python
from wenu.atlas_design import (
    AtlasPageGeometry, AtlasOverviewGeometry, design_band_atlas,
)

atlas = design_band_atlas(
    "b4-trial", 1,
    AtlasPageGeometry(353, 250, 10, 10, 20, 10),
    AtlasOverviewGeometry(join_ra_deg=82.5, shared_band_width_deg=20),
    field_width_deg=40, overlap_deg=2, seed_ra_deg=82.5,
)
atlas.write_json("atlas_band_trial_v1.json")
print(atlas.validation())
```

With these page/margin/overlap inputs, 30°, 40° and 50° fields give
**236, 128 and 80 sheets**. These are comparison cases, not accepted defaults.
The conservative placement uses the cap inside the shorter side of each
rectangle and therefore uses more sheets than a layout exploiting its full
width. An equatorial row includes the selected seed centre; the number of
sectors decreases toward the polar sheets. Numbering runs south to north,
with increasing RA from the seed in each row.

The JSON stores the resolved centres and primary ownership regions. Its
reader revalidates the whole-sphere partition and containment without
re-running placement. Primary regions partition the sky; full tangent-plane
rectangles include overlap. Shared-boundary neighbours are reciprocal; the
stored overlap is a conservative lower bound, not an actual intersection area.
The coverage check is analytic with a numerical guard, not a sampled-sky
claim or formal interval proof.

Changing overview join/band geometry preserves sheet centres, coverage and
IDs. Changing the page, field, overlap or placement seed requires a new
design revision and a new identifier namespace. Keep each trial JSON under
its own versioned filename and review coverage and sheet count before curation.
The prototype accepts stereographic fields from 5° to 120°, a remaining
inscribed cap of at least 1°, and at most 4,096 sheets; it fails explicitly
if the request cannot fit these bounds. It does not support arbitrary moved
centres or gnomonic tiling yet.

The two stereographic disks extend to the stored north/south limits. The join
meridian points inward on both disks. Numbers are the persisted editorial

For this first comparison, disks remain separate so no sky area is concealed

## Five-interior-band comparison (candidate)

A second Python layout follows the requested structure: one equatorial band,
two further bands per hemisphere, and one actual chart at each pole. It uses
the full B4 rectangles rather than an inscribed cap for interior coverage:

```python
from wenu.atlas_design import (
    AtlasPageGeometry, AtlasOverviewGeometry, design_five_band_atlas,
)
atlas = design_five_band_atlas(
    "b4-five-band", 1,
    AtlasPageGeometry(353, 250, 10, 10, 20, 10),
    AtlasOverviewGeometry(82.5, 20),
    field_width_deg=50, overlap_deg=0.25, seed_ra_deg=82.5,
    equatorial_half_height_deg=17, middle_boundary_dec_deg=45,
)
atlas.write_json("atlas_five_band_v1.json")
```

This trial has **34 sheets**: 8 equatorial, 7 in each inner sub-equatorial
band, 5 in each outer band and 2 polar. All useful rectangles are 323 × 230 mm
and share the same 50° central horizontal field/physical scale. Primary RA
intervals are 45°, 360/7° (about 51.43°) and 72°, respectively; these are
ownership widths, not the complete footprint's RA span at every declination.
The equatorial primary band runs from −17° to +17°, inner bands to ±45°,
outer bands to about ±72.1833°, and polar ownership covers the remaining caps.
These primary boundaries are not straight borders of the projected footprints.

The requested 0.25° (15 arcminutes) is a **minimum** overlap near every shared
primary boundary. Actual overlap varies across rectangular footprints and can
be larger, including near corners and poles. It is not a uniform 0.25° strip
everywhere. The new analytic check covers entire primary sectors and reserves
their angular neighbourhoods inside all four rectangle edges; display samples
do not establish the no-gap claim. Exactly contiguous bands and full RA
partitions cover the sphere. This is a guarded floating-point argument,
not formal interval arithmetic or a globally optimal tiling.

Render the same JSON with the existing review tool:

```bash
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py \
  atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_five_band_overview_v1
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py \
  atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_five_band_footprints_v1 --footprints
```

The earlier cap-based comparison stays available. This alternative requires
visual review before adopting its field, latitude boundaries or overlap.

## Composed astronomical index candidate (2026-10-09)

The geometry, separate-view plotter and five-band comparison were accepted,
merged and Mac-cleaned through PRs #216–#218 at main `cca31a82`. The following
candidate extends the same developer tool; only the designer command described below is installed; the plotter and
producer commands remain planned.

```bash
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py \\
  atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_joined_overview_v1 \\
  --joined --astronomy --star-magnitude-limit 5.5
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py \\
  atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_joined_footprints_v1 \\
  --joined --astronomy --star-magnitude-limit 5.5 --footprints
```

Each invocation reads and revalidates the resolved JSON, then writes PNG, PDF
and SVG under a new versioned prefix. Plotting does not rerun placement or
modify the JSON. Omitting `--joined` retains separate disks; omitting
`--astronomy` retains geometry-only review. The magnitude limit must be
finite, positive and at most 11.

The north disk is on the left and the south disk on the right. Equal physical
projection scales put the inward equatorial point at the JSON's join RA at
one common page position. Caps beyond the equator produce intersecting disk
contours. A vertical bisector chooses north content on the left and south
content on the right inside the paper lens. Each complete own hemisphere
survives this cut, so the composition preserves all-sky coverage. Both full
outer contours remain visible. A zero-width shared band gives tangent disks.

The common sky band is an RA-complete declination band, not the small lens on
the page. The two polar projections agree at the join point and cannot be
registered point for point throughout that band. Shared objects and primary
numbers can appear in both views. Sheet overlap, shared sky-band width and
page-contour intersection remain different quantities.

Optional astronomical content uses existing Hipparcos stars, western
constellation figures/abbreviations and D3-Celestial Milky Way rings through
`CelestialSphere.draw_chart()`. It uses native catalogue ICRS directions;
there is no observer, time, ephemeris, apparent-place calculation or proper-
motion propagation. Hipparcos coordinates/figure vertices/derived label
anchors retain their native J1991.25 catalogue epoch; morphology carries no
stellar epoch. This is an orientation index, with the existing catalogue and
source provenance, not a current-date observing chart. Faint constellation
vertices remain available to figures independently of the stellar marker cut.
The filled Milky Way preserves source ring holes and handedness. Index
appearance and density do not alter the B4 chart layout or coverage certificate.


The accepted lower-density index can be reproduced with stars through magnitude
4.5, the faint OL1 Milky Way envelope added to OL2–OL5, and both Magellanic
Clouds. These are optional plotting choices; the default remains magnitude
5.5 with OL2–OL5 and no Clouds. Use a new versioned output prefix:

```bash
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_joined_overview_mag45_v2 --joined --astronomy --star-magnitude-limit 4.5 --include-lowest-mw-isophote --magellanic-clouds
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py atlas_five_band_v1.json atlas_joined_footprints_mag45_v2 --joined --astronomy --footprints --star-magnitude-limit 4.5 --include-lowest-mw-isophote --magellanic-clouds
```

`--include-lowest-mw-isophote` and `--magellanic-clouds` require `--astronomy`.
The Clouds use all four levels from each existing Gaia-derived source snapshot,
with their source rings, holes and compound grouping retained. Their native ICRS
morphology is realized in the existing Magellanic Cloud layer, with no observer
or assigned epoch; the usual observed rendering remains available. The same
resolved JSON and its chart IDs, scale, primary regions and coverage are used.


## Installed geometry designer candidate

`wenu_design_atlas --config REQUEST_TOML --output NEW_JSON` is the installed
adapter for the existing complete stereographic band designers. It produces
only one resolved JSON, with diagnostics on stderr. It does not produce a
figure, read a catalogue, construct an observer or use a network service.
This milestone exposes the supported geometry methods; it does not adopt the
example's page/profile values as universal defaults or finish atlas publication.

The repository example `examples/atlas_design_request_v1.toml` reproduces the
accepted 34-sheet B4 layout. After updating the editable installation, run:

```bash
wenu_design_atlas --config examples/atlas_design_request_v1.toml --output /tmp/atlas_design_v1.json
```

The module route `python -m wenu.cli.atlas` accepts the same arguments.
Choose a new versioned destination for each review. The output parent must
already exist. Existing files and symlinks are rejected, including a file that
appears during publication. The complete JSON is published atomically; failed
validation produces no JSON and publication failures remove temporary files.
Ordinary input/filesystem failures exit 2 with a concise error; interruption
exits 130. There is no overwrite flag. Input TOML is left unchanged.

### Version-1 request contract

This is a separate atlas protocol, not a schema-v2 `wenu_chart` overlay.
All keys are closed: missing required keys, unknown keys and duplicate TOML
definitions are errors. Every geometric number must be finite, with no numeric
strings or Boolean substitutes. The top-level fields are:

| Field/table | Required content |
|---|---|
| `schema_version` | Integer `1` |
| `document_kind` | `"wenu-atlas-design-request"` |
| `design_id`, `revision` | Nonempty trimmed identity; positive integer revision |
| `coordinate_frame`, `projection` | `"icrs"`, `"stereographic"` |
| `[page]` | `width_mm`, `height_mm`, `top_mm`, `bottom_mm`, `inner_mm`, `outer_mm` |
| `[overview]` | Exactly one of `join_ra_deg` or `join_ra_hours`, plus `shared_band_width_deg` |
| `[tiling]` | `algorithm`, `field_width_deg`, `overlap_deg`, exactly one of `seed_ra_deg` or `seed_ra_hours`; optional `max_sheets` (default 4096) |

RA is in [0,360) degrees or [0,24) hours; explicit hours convert by 15 degrees
per hour. Persisted JSON always uses degrees. Page width/height are positive;
margins are nonnegative and must leave a positive useful rectangle. The field
is the full central horizontal angular span in [5,120] degrees. Height follows
from the projected useful-paper aspect, not direct angular scaling. Minimum
sheet overlap is nonnegative degrees; it is independent of the overview's
total shared band in [0,180) degrees and of the physical index lens.

Algorithm `five_band` additionally requires `equatorial_half_height_deg` and
`middle_boundary_dec_deg`. Ordered positive boundaries must fit the resolved
polar extent and sheet budget. Algorithm `cap_bands` rejects these profile
keys and chooses its own latitude-band count using the accepted inscribed-cap
method. Infeasible requests fail instead of returning a partial atlas.

Interior sheets are north up with RA increasing left; the seed orients polar
sheets and places a centre in every interior row. The overview join RA does
not control placement. Numbering runs south to north and increasing RA from
the seed within each row. Arbitrary orientations, editorial reorderings,
free-centre layouts, gnomonic projection and other coordinate frames are not
supported by request version 1; they require a later contract.

### Resolved output and subsequent plotting

Output retains the existing schema-v1 `wenu-atlas-band-tiling` format without
adding a second wrapper. It stores exact resolved sheet geometry, physical
page, primary bands, overview geometry, seed/overlap, versioned generator
method, reciprocal neighbours and recomputable validation. It contains no
generated timestamp. Keep the generating TOML alongside the JSON; the JSON
does not embed the original TOML bytes or its processing-only sheet budget.
Equivalent hours/degrees inputs produce identical JSON. The embedded geometry
specimen remains unverified in isolation; whole-sphere evidence belongs to
the outer band-tiling envelope. This is guarded analytic floating-point
validation, not formal interval certification or globally optimal tiling.

The unchanged developer tool reads this output directly, without rerunning
placement. For example:

```bash
python tools/render_atlas_band_overview_v1.py /tmp/atlas_design_v1.json /tmp/atlas_index_v1 --joined --astronomy --star-magnitude-limit 4.5 --include-lowest-mw-isophote --magellanic-clouds
```

The installed `wenu_plot_atlas` adapter is the next milestone; individual
chart/report pairs and their publication inputs follow separately.


## Installed index plotter candidate

`wenu_plot_atlas --design RESOLVED_JSON --config PRESENTATION_TOML --output-prefix NEW_PREFIX`
reads and revalidates the existing design. It never reruns placement, changes
sheet identity/numbers or rewrites JSON. Export a joined index using the authored
`examples/atlas_index_style_v1.toml`:

```bash
wenu_plot_atlas --design /tmp/atlas_design_v1.json \
  --config examples/atlas_index_style_v1.toml \
  --output-prefix /tmp/atlas_index_v1
```

Use a fresh prefix with no extension and an existing parent directory. The
command prints the produced paths. Requested existing files and symlinks are
preserved. All formats render in a temporary directory before no-clobber
publication; render failure, a concurrent destination or interruption cleans
this invocation's outputs. Multiple-file publication is not a transactional
filesystem operation across a process crash.

The closed schema requires `schema_version = 1`,
`document_kind = "wenu-atlas-index-presentation"` and every field below.
Unknown keys, duplicate list entries, missing fields and unsupported versions
are rejected; schema-v2 chart configuration is a different input.

| Table | Required fields and meaning |
|---|---|
| `layout` | `joined`, `footprints`: booleans; `width_mm`, `height_mm`: figure dimensions from 50 to 1,000 mm. These do not change atlas-sheet paper geometry. |
| `content` | `layers`: any subset of `stars`, `constellation_lines`, `constellation_labels`, `milky_way`, `magellanic_clouds`; an empty list makes a geometry-only index. `star_magnitude_limit`: finite −2 through 11. `milky_way_levels`: a unique subset of `ol1`–`ol5`, nonempty exactly when Milky Way is enabled. |
| `typography` | `heading_size_pt`, `number_size_pt`, `text_size_pt`: 2–72 pt. These control index headings, sheet numbers and explanatory text. Astronomical labels use the canonical atlas style. |
| `colours` | `paper_color`, `ink_color`, `border_color`, `highlight_color`, `guide_color`, `footprint_color`: Matplotlib colour strings controlling index furniture and overlays; catalogue layers use canonical atlas styling. |
| `export` | `formats`: a nonempty unique subset of `png`, `pdf`, `svg`; `dpi`: integer 72–1,200; `transparent`: boolean for figure background. No tight crop, so physical figure dimensions remain fixed. |

The supplied example retains the accepted joined composition, magnitude 4.5,
OL1–OL5, both Clouds and footprint overlay. The join RA, shared sky band and all
34 numbers come from JSON; layout controls their figure presentation. A joined
pair bisects the paper lens while preserving both complete contour guides.
Separate disks use `joined = false`. Primary borders exclude sheet overlap;
`footprints = true` adds the sampled complete sheet boundaries.

Native Hipparcos J1991.25 directions and fixed ICRS morphology retain their
source identities, without observer, ephemeris or epoch propagation. Lines and
abbreviations need catalogue vertices internally even when stars are hidden.
IAU constellation boundaries and moving/observer layers are not admitted by
this first index presentation schema. They require their own coordinate seam.
The earlier developer tool remains a compatibility adapter to this same owner.
Chart/report-page production is the next separate milestone.


### Configurable celestial veil

The optional `[veil]` table contains exactly `enabled` (boolean), `color`
(a Matplotlib colour string) and `opacity` (finite number from 0 to 1).
Opacity 0 is completely transparent; 1 hides the celestial objects completely.
Omitting this table disables the veil, preserving version-1 input compatibility.

`examples/atlas_index_style_v2.toml` retains the preceding astronomical choices
and adds a white veil with opacity 0.55. It remains a schema-version-1 input;
`v2` versions this authored example and its changed appearance.

The veil is drawn inside each projected cap above celestial objects and below
primary borders, footprint overlays, guides and sheet numbers. It follows the
joined-lens clipping and export DPI, leaving outer paper and explanatory text
unchanged. It has no effect on geometry-only indices, disabled/zero-opacity
views or the source JSON. Use a fresh output such as `atlas_index_v2`.

### Atlas SVG hierarchy

The index declares semantic identities before export. Each hemisphere contains
celestial content (existing catalogue layers, constellations and isophotes), a
veil, atlas charts keyed by JSON chart number, guides and its heading. Global
furniture names the title, subtitle and legends. For example,
`chart-14-primary-boundary-north`, `chart-14-footprint-south` and
`chart-14-number-label-north` carry `data-chart-number` and `data-role`.
Star marker instances use catalogue HIP identifiers, such as
`star-hip-24436-south`; repeated decorative marker overlays have distinct IDs.
Constellation and morphology identities retain their canonical source keys
with a hemisphere suffix. Every SVG ID is unique across the paired index.

Editor groups expose one **North Disk** and one **South Disk**. Each contains
Stars (individual HIP markers), Constellations (one group per canonical
abbreviation, with Lines and Label), Milky Way, optional Magellanic Clouds,
Veil, Guides, Charts and Heading. Each chart occurs once per visible disk:
`Charts → Chart 21 → Primary Boundary / Full Footprint / Label`.
Boundaries are directly editable paths. The Label retains only its editable
Text and white Background, preserving the number's backing box; anonymous
backend wrappers and repeated semantic ancestors are removed. A chart wholly
outside a disk does not create an empty footprint or chart group.

Chart furniture is drawn together, in ascending chart-number order, above the
veil and guides in every format. Within each chart the primary boundary is
followed by its full footprint and number. Constellation lines and labels are
also grouped together, below star symbols. This explicitly declared order

The stellar key remains specific to each chart. Its first integer is the floor
of the brightest plotted magnitude, so it is at least as bright as every
plotted star: 1.1 starts at 1; -1.46 starts at -2. Its last integer is the ceiling

## Measure star sizes from atlas SVG files

The offline script reads existing SVGs; it does not regenerate charts, load a

```

```

Directory inputs select only `chart-NN.svg`, excluding report pages. Explicit
SVG inputs may also be atlas indexes. Duplicate input paths are removed. The
output directory must exist; the output prefix must be new. The script writes
PNG, PDF and SVG plots, `_stars.csv` (one row per marker), `_bins.csv` (bin
boundaries and counts), and `_summary.json` (per-file counts and conventions).

Both axes use base-10 logarithms. Counts are appearances per equal-width bin
in log area, not cumulative counts or number per square millimetre. Empty bins
remain in the CSV but have no log-count point. Overlapping charts count repeated
appearances separately; no unique-star identification or catalogue cross-match
is inferred.

Area is the full filled SVG silhouette in square millimetres of the printed
page, including inherited affine transforms and physical page scaling. It is
not the Matplotlib scatter parameter `s`, the stellar flux, or angular area.
Strokes, labels, legend markers and variable/multiple classification overlays
are excluded. Bright filled star markers are included. Zero-area and hidden
markers are excluded. A marker must have its centre inside the page and its
SVG clips; a partly clipped marker retains its full symbol area.

The supported Wenu vector subset comprises line, quadratic and cubic Bezier
paths, circles, local marker references and affine transforms. Unsupported
marker path commands, malformed/cyclic references, nonuniform root viewport
scaling and unsupported clip shapes fail explicitly. Rasterized stellar layers
without vector markers cannot provide these measurements. Use the original
Wenu exports; arbitrary CSS, masks, filters and complex editor geometry are
not an SVG-renderer compatibility promise.

Magnitude references are added automatically when all chart SVGs have matching
SHA-256 entries in adjacent atlas batch `manifest.json` files and share the
`atlas` print style, diameter multiplier and magnitude limit. Vertical dashed
lines use Wenu's canonical magnitude sizing and circular marker geometry;
magnitudes are annotated along the top. Older manifests without
`star_size_scale` use the original multiplier `1.0`. The minimum symbol area
collapses a range of faint magnitudes to one position: for the current atlas
preset and a limit of 8, that line is labeled `5.50–8`. It does not imply that
individual magnitudes can be recovered from those identically sized symbols.
The guide values and their availability status are saved in the summary JSON.

Missing manifests, edited SVGs or incompatible presentations retain the area
histogram but omit magnitude guides, with an explicit status. Use
`--no-magnitude-guides` to disable them. Resolving these appearance references
does not create a sky or reload catalogues.

## Shared stellar-sizing experiments

Stellar sizing is shared with regional, binocular, all-sky and planisphere
charts. `StellarMagnitudeSizing` selects `legacy` (unchanged exponential scatter
area with clamps), `linear` (diameter linear in magnitude difference), or
`asinh` (diameter with smooth bright-end compression), or `quadratic`
(diameter quadratic in magnitude difference). The new curves are Wenu
experimental policies, not implementations attributed to Toshimi Taki.

For the new laws, define `t = max(0, size_reference_magnitude - m)`:

- linear: `d_mm = dmin_mm + a_mm * t`;
- asinh: `d_mm = dmin_mm + a_mm * asinh(t / b_mag)`;
- quadratic: `d_mm = dmin_mm + a_mm * t**2`.

Scatter size is `(72 * d_mm / 25.4)^2`; the recorded circular vector geometry
converts that size to filled area. The minimum belongs to the sizing reference,
not necessarily the selection limit. Set a reference of 8 for both magnitude-6
and magnitude-8 comparisons. The selection limit must not exceed the reference;
otherwise the experiment would reintroduce an in-range faint-size plateau.

Shared `wenu_chart` switches are `--mag-algorithm`, `--mag-dmin` (mm),
`--mag-a` (mm), `--mag-b` (magnitudes, used by asinh), and
`--mag-size-reference`. The atlas command accepts the same switches, plus
`--style atlas|cartoon`, `--mode print|presentation` and `--star-size-scale`.
The latter is an explicit diameter multiplier. Use `--star-size-scale 1` when
comparing literal millimetre parameters; the existing example requests 1.5.
Physical diameter overrides replace historical preset/family area factors and
polar magnitude remapping. They use circular stellar glyphs; classification
marks keep their existing separate ownership. No automatic density-based
calibration is claimed by this experimental milestone.

The atlas presentation TOML also accepts `[stellar_magnitude_sizing]` with
`algorithm`, `dmin_mm`, `a_mm`, `b_mag`, and `size_reference_magnitude`.
General schema-v2 chart configuration accepts `mag_algorithm`, `mag_dmin`,
`mag_a`, `mag_b` and `mag_size_reference` under `[styles.atlas.stars]` or
`[styles.cartoon.stars]`. CLI overrides take precedence. The existing
`--magnitude-limit` controls selection independently.

Each completed atlas now includes a final `stellar-sizing-report.pdf` page
in `atlas.pdf`, plus `stellar-sizing-report.png`, `.svg` and `_data.json`.
The report contains log-count/log-area measurements with magnitude guides,
log-area versus magnitude (magnitude itself is already logarithmic), the exact
resolved formula/parameters, page/style/mode information and actual-size symbol
samples. `stellar-sizing.{png,pdf,svg}` and `_stars.csv`, `_bins.csv`,
`_summary.json` provide the standalone histogram and measured data. SVG charts
are retained even for PDF-only requests so later analysis is self-contained.
The final manifest records the resolved policy, marker geometry, paper
geometry and hashes; analysis uses those values instead of current presets.
Older manifests retain their documented atlas-print compatibility route.

Create one saved sphere using `--save-sky`; reuse it with `--from-sky` for every
subsequent sizing/style/selection experiment within snapshot coverage. No new
projection cache or second sky product is introduced. Index presentation remains
independent and is not regenerated by the chart command.

Use direct commands and new output directories, for example:

The PDF budget page follows the sizing report in the book. Each occupied
one-magnitude interval `[lower, upper)` includes its count and summed filled
circular symbol area. Fractions use the actual rendered sky area, excluding
page margins and the coordinate-label frame. Exact integer magnitudes enter the interval starting at that
integer. Counts are appearances: charts overlapping on the sky repeat stars.
Areas exclude stroke width and do not compute the union of overlapping disks.

The analyzer verifies report hashes, policy, total count and area against
measured SVGs. Historical atlases lacking exact budgets still receive sizing
analysis, with an explicit unavailable budget status; magnitudes are never
inferred from symbol size. Counts inform review and do not change the law.

### Chart magnitude key and facing-page interval report

Atlas charts now show their magnitude key inside the chart frame. Its integer
references bracket all plotted stars: floor(brightest) through ceil(faintest).
There are no counts in this compact key. Symbol diameters use the exact chart
law and diameter multiplier; no star magnitudes are quantized by the key.

The facing report shows the full reference scale, normally -2 through the
physical sizing reference (or at least 8 for legacy). Each symbol at m is
labelled `m ≤ magnitude < m+1` and gives the number of plotted stars in that
interval. The displayed symbol is evaluated at its bright bound m; actual
star diameters vary continuously within the interval. This is an interval count, not a cumulative count. Exact integer
magnitudes enter the interval starting at that integer. A dotted box surrounds
the same reference range used on the chart. Remaining rows receive a veil;
configure `magnitude_veil_color = "white"` and `magnitude_veil_alpha = 0.65`
in the atlas chart presentation TOML. Alpha 0 disables the veil; 1 hides
inactive rows completely. The complete scale and counts remain in report JSON.
Empty charts omit the stellar keys. Counts come from prepared visible-star
geometry, including the final footprint, without rebuilding the sphere.

The legend's marker column and individual row heights accommodate physical
symbol diameters at the selected scale; the largest symbols no longer spill
outside the frame or collide with adjacent references. Chart and report
legend exports follow the same backend/DPI-aware shared furniture path.

## Coordinate grid and readable edge markings

Atlas chart pages use fixed ICRS grid lines every **2h in RA** by default and **10° in
declination**. Edge marks follow the local projected coordinate curve into the
sky. Every whole hour is labelled; optional **20m and 40m** labels use smaller text; **10m,
30m and 50m** have marks only. Declination marks and labels occur every **10°**
where the corresponding curve crosses a page's sky boundary. A curve entirely
inside the sky has no edge crossing. The poles have no unique RA label.

Optional chart-presentation keys (same schema version 1):

```toml
grid_lines_visible = true
grid_ra_step_hours = 2
grid_minute_labels_visible = false
milky_way_color = "#87CEFA"
grid_label_fontsize = 12.0
grid_minor_label_scale = 0.75
grid_frame_width_scale = 1.5
```

Font sizes are physical points: the defaults give 12-point hour/declination
labels and 9-point minute labels. The nominal exterior-frame minimum is 50%
wider; font measurement reserves extra space for overlapping labels. Dense
minute/declination labels move outward into separate lanes while their marks
stay at the true coordinate crossing. The sky shrinks uniformly within the
useful paper margins, retaining the exact JSON projection, angular coverage and
sheet numbering. Stellar area fractions use the actual reduced sky area.

Set `grid_lines_visible = false` to retain edge marks and labels with the
interior lines hidden. Remove `equatorial_grid` from `layers` to omit the
complete equatorial grid and its reserved frame space. Both controls are
independent of the resolved atlas design and saved sky data.

`wenu_create_atlas` renders every chart through the official `wenu_chart`
entry, using its fixed-ICRS `atlas-sheet` command in process. The producer
prepares or verifies the sky once; each worker reuses its mapped sky. Book
assembly and index handling remain with the atlas command.

To render one sheet independently from an existing sufficient, design-bound
saved sky (use a new output directory):

```bash
wenu_chart atlas-sheet \
  --design atlas_portrait_design_v1.json \
  --config examples/atlas_chart_style_v2.toml \
  --from-sky atlas_portrait_sky_v1 \
  --chart-number 1 --output-dir atlas_single_sheet_v1
```

This writes the selected chart, its report and report data in the presentation's
formats. It checks the saved sky against the exact design bytes and requested
layers/magnitude limit. It does not assemble a complete atlas book.

Ordinary regional charts can use the same markings with
`wenu_chart regional ... --equatorial-grid-ticks`. Appearance options are
`--grid-label-fontsize`, `--grid-minor-label-scale`,
`--grid-frame-width-scale` and `--grid-lines-visible`/`--no-grid-lines-visible`.
Use `--no-equatorial-grid-ticks` to override an enabled TOML preset. Regional
charts retain their observer-time coordinate realization and original viewport;
atlas sheets retain their fixed ICRS design. The preset currently requires a
regional chart with its equatorial grid and exterior label band enabled.


RA unit letters use editable Unicode superscripts (ʰ and ᵐ). Declination labels
are parallel to the local projected declination curve at its boundary crossing, with upright text
orientation. Visible grid lines use black at 0.65 opacity and 0.55-point width.
The portrait review set is charts 1, 5, 8, 10, 11 and 22, covering both polar
caps and the requested Orion, Virgo and Scorpius fields. Recreate its index
with wenu_plot_atlas and examples/atlas_index_style_v2.toml to export PNG, SVG
and PDF together.

Set grid_ra_step_hours to 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 12 to change full RA grid lines.
Minute labels are hidden by default; grid_minute_labels_visible=true restores
20m/40m labels while retaining every 10m tick. Ordinary regional equivalents
are --grid-ra-step-hours and --grid-minute-labels-visible (with a negative form).
Dec text follows the extrapolated curve outward from the unchanged boundary
crossing. milky_way_color sets the same pale sky-blue fill for MW, LMC and SMC.
The index presentation accepts the same optional key under [colours]. The index
PNG/SVG/PDF are independent files beside the chart output directory; open their
parent folder to inspect all three.

When a dedicated galaxy, open-cluster, globular-cluster, planetary-nebula or SNR
layer is enabled, it owns that category: generic Messier dotted outlines are
suppressed for the category. Disabling its dedicated layer restores the generic
outline. This removes cluster-like circles over dedicated galaxy symbols and
other duplicate category drawings without changing saved source catalogues.
